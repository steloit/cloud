# Catalog Structure Review — Model A (independent products) vs Model B (database modules)

**Status:** Founder-requested research review · 2026-07-18 · Four parallel web-research passes (hyperscaler/PaaS catalogs · developer-platform catalogs · developer search psychology · BYOD landscape), all facts verified against live sources this week. Full agent reports preserved in session; citations inline below are the load-bearing subset.
**Verdict up front:** **The evidence confirms the ratified hybrid (ADR-038/039/040) — Model A at the catalog plane, Model B at the architecture plane, the Composer as the bridge — and it does so more strongly than the ratification itself argued.** The market's two fastest-growing platforms sit at the two poles, but the pole nearest Steloit's shape (Supabase: modules-on-one-Postgres, $2B → $10.5B in ~12 months) is also the one whose *presentation* mistakes Steloit's two-plane rule already avoids. Four refinements fall out of the evidence (pricing of in-database capabilities, Scheduler folded into Jobs, console discoverability, agent-native provisioning). **BYOD is rejected on evidence** for the capability products at launch. Under ADR-040's stability clause this review is *evidence arriving and confirming* — no architecture changes.

---

## 1 · What the market evidence shows (condensed)

**Three catalog archetypes are in live competition:**
- **Modules-on-one-database** (Supabase; Neon converging on it July 2026): Supabase at ~$10.5B/​~$170M ARR; Queues and Cron shipped as "Postgres Modules" (`/modules/` URL path, pgmq/pg_cron credited in the subtitle), free inside the database, absent from the pricing page entirely — explicitly positioned as "eliminates RabbitMQ/Kafka," i.e. retention, not revenue.
- **Independent metered primitives** (Cloudflare, Upstash; Vercel's new Queues/Workflow): Cloudflare thrives at 5.5M Workers developers — but behind ONE $5 gate, with cron *included free*, and the Worker (not the catalog) as the composition point. Upstash is the small-scale control case: ~$11.9M total raised, no Series B found, Kafka shut down (paying users cut off), Search in preview 13+ months — standalone primitives without an anchor show weak momentum, re-confirming ADR-0004's dead-category evidence.
- **Outcome suite under one plan** (Firebase): validated discoverability, but the bill-shock canon ($30k/72h, $72k Milkie Way, "$70k in one day" 2025) all trace to per-op metering with no hard cap — the exact failure Steloit's cap is built against.

**The hyperscaler model (the founder's "Model A" reference) is in documented retreat:** AWS's ~240-service list is an artifact of two-pizza-team mitosis (AWS's own words), maintained by official "which of our overlapping products do I use" docs — the existence of which is evidence the catalog exceeds a composable mental model; 2024–26 brought three AWS sunset waves, Azure retiring its entire Redis cache line, Google consolidating Vertex AI away. **Jobs/cron is a standalone paid product nowhere below hyperscaler scale** — Render/Railway make it a free platform primitive, Heroku's Scheduler is a free best-effort add-on, Fly ships DIY blueprints, DigitalOcean buried it as an App Platform feature.

**Capabilities are migrating INTO the database industry-wide:** pgvector on every managed Postgres; Google markets AlloyDB ScaNN as "you don't need a separate vector database"; DigitalOcean sells Kafka/OpenSearch/Valkey as engines of ONE Managed Databases product; Heroku pgvector free. The 2025 money agreed: Databricks paid ~$1B for Neon, Snowflake ~$250M for Crunchy, while Pinecone sought a buyer below its 2023 valuation.

**Neon's July 2026 restructure is convergent evolution toward the ratified model:** "Database, Auth, Functions, Object Storage, AI Gateway — the Postgres backend for apps and agents," every primitive legitimized by its relation to the database ("branches with it," "next to your data"). Adjacent products, everywhere, are sold by their relationship to the anchor — never as independent categories.

## 2 · Developer psychology (the vocabulary evidence)

Developers **ask in outcome words and accept infrastructure words as the answer**: the 600–760-point HN debates are titled "Choose Postgres queue technology" / "Just use Postgres" (outcome-framed questions, infra-framed answers); every pgmq-as-such HN submission ever scored ≤10 points; "Supabase Cron" got zero HN stories; no vendor leads a landing page with an extension name — extension names appear one level down as *trust signals* ("built on pgmq"). The market synthesis is exactly ADR-039's two planes: **outcome name on top, architecture name honestly one level down.**

The pgvector/Pinecone case sets the seam precedent: in-database wins defaults below scale thresholds ("pgvector default under ~5M vectors"); AlloyDB swapped in Google's ScaNN engine *under the pgvector interface* — engine evolution inside the capability, the MongoDB-Atlas seam, live in Postgres.

**The AI-era shift moves discovery from keyword search to toolchain defaults:** >80% of Neon databases are agent-created; >60% of new Supabase databases come from AI tools (Claude Code the largest single contributor since 2026); Lovable/Bolt/v0 default to Supabase. The builder, not the developer, increasingly picks the backend — and no major builder defaults to a standalone queue/search/vector product; they default to Postgres-platform bundles.

## 3 · Technical independence (Q4 answered)

Could Jobs/Scheduler/Search/Vector migrate off Postgres without changing the product? **The evidence repeats ADR-038's answer: only where semantics are backend-invariant.** Transactional enqueue/branch-coherence die in any move out of the customer's database (the HN queue debates name transactional enqueue as *the* killer feature). The correct portability is the Atlas/AlloyDB seam — engine evolves *inside* the capability, interface and state-location stable. Treating them as independent products is the Upstash/AWS shape, with the momentum and retreat evidence above. **Treating them as products at the *catalog* plane while keeping them capabilities at the *architecture* plane — the ratified two-plane rule — is what the winning platforms converged on** (Supabase's marketing products vs `/modules/` truth; Neon's primitives defined by database-relation).

## 4 · BYOD (Q5 answered: no, for the capability products, at launch)

- **Technical:** pgmq is NOT on the extension allow-lists of RDS, Cloud SQL, or Azure (SQL-only self-managed install is the escape hatch); pg_cron requires a customer-console parameter change + reboot on all three hyperscalers — an enablement step the platform cannot perform; Neon runs pg_cron only while compute is awake. Everything wedge-critical is substrate-bound: CoW branching (Neon/Xata/Lakebase are storage-layer; Aurora cloning is capped at 15, same-region, AWS-API-only), scale-to-zero, PITR-as-product, masked-data previews. Supabase's own capabilities empirically break on locked-down external databases (the 2-weeks-of-CloudFormation operator reports).
- **Commercial:** the 2025–26 pattern is one-directional — substrate owners were acquired at premiums (Neon ~$1B, Crunchy ~$250M); pure connect-to-your-DB products became free platform glue (Hyperdrive), repriced three times and pivoted (Hasura → PromptQL), or shut down (Tembo — pgmq's own creator). Prisma, a BYO-DB company, built its own managed Postgres and stated why: reselling hyperscaler infra breaks the economics.
- **Steloit-specific:** estimate-before-provision, the enforced cap, and metering are **unpriceable and unenforceable on infrastructure Steloit doesn't control** — BYOD wouldn't dilute the wedge, it would delete it. Credential custody for customers' production RDS is the CircleCI/Heroku-breach threat class.
- **Door left open (evidence-gated, not now):** the Binding primitive already models external state; a future read-only "connect your existing Postgres" for onboarding/migration (Hyperdrive-shaped: free glue, credentials wrapped, never the capability substrate) can enter through the gate if partner evidence demands it. The Inngest pattern (state on our side, code anywhere) is the other lawful shape — it never touches the customer DB at all.

## 5 · Four refinements the evidence adds (implementation-plane; no frozen surface moves)

1. **Pricing of in-database capabilities — include, don't meter.** Supabase ships Queues/Cron at $0 inside the database; Cloudflare includes cron in the $5 gate; nobody below hyperscaler scale sells scheduling. Refinement to the ADR-040 pricing sketch: **Jobs, Cron, basic Search, and Vector are included with the Database at no separate line** — they consume the database's own metered capacity (compute/storage the customer already sees priced), with plan-level allowances where abuse is possible. This is *stronger* certainty (fewer meters = fewer bill-shock surfaces; the per-op-metering ↔ bill-shock correlation is documented across Firebase/Vercel/Neon/Supabase complaints) and matches the "eliminate the second vendor" retention logic. Feeds the pending ADR-022 amendment.
2. **Scheduler is not a catalog entry — it's inside Jobs.** GCP needs an official comparison doc for its three scheduling products; Supabase splits Queues/Cron into two modules and users file discoverability issues anyway. One intent — **Jobs (background + scheduled work)** — with cron as a mode. State Test: pg_cron state is of the database → capability; no new surface.
3. **Console discoverability is a first-class requirement, not an afterthought.** The observed anti-pattern: Supabase's marketing sells Queues/Cron as products while the dashboard buries them under "Integrations" ("Cannot find Cron Job UI" issues); on every platform examined, dashboard taxonomy lags marketing taxonomy. ADR-039's console rule (outcome reads first, architecture inspectable) must bind the E8-S3 create canvas AND the service workspace tabs — the catalog story and the in-console IA are one artifact, not two.
4. **Agent-native provisioning is a discovery channel, not a nice-to-have.** With >60–80% of new databases created by agents/builders on the leading platforms, the Composer's intent → priced-resolutions API is exactly agent-shaped — and estimate-before-provision is *more* valuable to an agent's operator than to a human (agents are the bill-shock generator). Roadmap note for E13: expose the Composer via MCP/API early; "the platform agents can price" is a wedge extension, not a new product.

## 6 · Risks

- **Trust cost of catalog churn** (documented on every platform that did it: Upstash Kafka, Firebase Dynamic Links/Studio, Neon Auth v1/pg_search, PlanetScale Hobby/Boost): never launch a catalog entry that might be revoked — this is the evidence backing ADR-040's evidence-gated roadmap intents. Ship eight real things, not eight promises.
- **Supabase's gravity:** the modules-on-Postgres lane has a $10.5B incumbent with builder-default distribution. Steloit does not out-breadth it (dx-first-review evidence, reconciled); it differentiates on certainty (priced/capped/masked previews) — the one thing absent from every complaint-thread-generating platform surveyed.
- **The two-plane rule's own failure mode:** planes drifting apart (the Supabase marketing-vs-dashboard gap). Mitigation is refinement 3 + the intent tag as the single structural key.

## 7 · Disposition

Filed as confirming evidence under ADR-040's stability clause. No ADR supersession proposed. Refinements 1–4 route to: ADR-022 amendment (founder stamp, pending), E8-S3 spec, E13 roadmap note, and the S9 reconciliation pass. BYOD recorded as evidence-rejected with the Binding-shaped door noted for the M4 checkpoint's partner questions ("do partners ask to connect an existing RDS?" is now a pre-registered question worth adding).
