# Developer-First Platform — Constitutional Review (four-plane synthesis)

**Status:** Founder review · 2026-07-18 · One of two independent reviews of the same founder decision; the companion review (verdict: Path A — principle-as-restatement + a pre-registered M4 pivot checkpoint) was reconciled into this document (§7) and removed at founder direction. This review reaches the ratified landing zone: adopt the principle as a *constitutional promotion* of what ADR-038/039 already ratified, with three corrections to the decision as originally posed. The founder accepted the corrections and added four refinements (below). Ratified as ADR-039 (§4b–4c of `product-taxonomy-review.md`) + ADR-040 (this document's §5).

---

## 1 · Verdict

The principle — *Steloit is a Developer-First Platform; infrastructure serves the developer experience, it does not define it* — is directionally right, and ~70% of it was already ratified constitution before the decision was written: ADR-038's UX law ("the catalog sells intents; the composer resolves them; dependencies satisfied by composition, never homework"), the §4b two-plane rule, messaging.md's "Branching is how; it is never why," ADR-002's "deploy your app, not buy a database," and D8's grammar-only surface. **What the decision actually does is promote the catalog plane from a UX law to the constitutional organizing principle.** That promotion is correct and cheap: the resource model, API tree, Product enum, substrate, and wedge all survive untouched.

## 2 · The three corrections (accepted by the founder)

1. **Semantic contracts, not API signatures, are the abstraction boundary.** "Execution must evolve without changing the developer-facing API, or the abstraction is wrong" is Model B, refuted with evidence in ADR-038: transactional enqueue and branch-coherence cannot survive pgmq→Kafka; FTS→OpenSearch loses transactional freshness. Where semantics are backend-invariant (flags evaluation, blob storage, AI routing), execution evolves freely and silently. Where semantics are the differentiation, evolution happens *inside* the capability scoped to the same state (the MongoDB-Atlas seam) or ships as a differently-named variant the customer consciously picks. **The invariant: execution models are replaceable; semantic contracts are not.**
2. **The Composer proposes; it never silently decides.** A Composer that changes execution models on its own judgment breaks estimate-before-provision. The accepted estimate is the reconciliation contract between the catalog plane and the architecture plane; execution-model migrations are visible, priced, consented events. (AI Law 1 applied to provisioning.)
3. **The catalog re-fronts existing goods; it does not launch eight products.** ADR-0004's dead-category evidence and D11's one-path alpha stand. Catalog v1 is a presentation re-front of already-ratified surface; new intents (Feature Flags, Auth, Realtime, Analytics, Streams) enter only through the gate, on evidence.

Plus one correction in the other direction: **"not a PostgreSQL cloud" is half-true.** No catalog entry exists *because* the infrastructure exists — but an infrastructure name may be a catalog entry when it *is* the outcome developers ask for. Database (PostgreSQL) stays on the shelf; Valkey presents as Cache; Worker folds into App and Jobs.

## 3 · The founder's four refinements (2026-07-18, adopted)

1. **Execution models are an extensible concept, never a closed list.** Constitutional rule (founder wording): *"A Product declares developer intent. The Composer resolves that intent into one or more execution models. Execution models may evolve over time without changing the Product abstraction, provided the semantic contract is preserved or a new semantic variant is introduced."* The four known models today — in-database capability · dedicated service · shared platform service · external binding — are instances, not the definition.
2. **"Execution models are replaceable. Semantic contracts are not"** becomes a named core design principle — the one sentence explaining why Search cannot silently become OpenSearch while Feature Flags could move between implementations invisibly.
3. **The Developer-First test** — every new product proposal answers, before any implementation discussion: (1) What developer problem does this solve? (2) Is this a Product, Capability, Binding, or Infrastructure Service (the State Test answers this)? (3) What execution model provides the best developer experience? (4) Does this strengthen the certainty story? Prepended to the new-managed-product gate.
4. **Architecture stability after ADR-040.** From ratification forward, architecture changes only on implementation evidence, performance evidence, customer feedback, or security requirements — *a cleaner abstraction is never a trigger* (ADR-0001's measured-trigger rule extended to product architecture). The next phase is validation with real developers and building the Composer, the estimate flow, and the developer experience.

## 4 · The four-plane model

1. **Products (catalog plane)** — flat, outcome-named intents; parentage never displayed as structure; each entry states its semantic contract (what the state is, what it branches with, what the meter is). **Live at v1** (all backed by ratified decisions or built surface): App · Database—PostgreSQL · Jobs · Search · Vector · Cache · Storage (Binding) · AI (Binding; A5.4 stands — the intent resolves to governed provider connection, never a proxy). **Roadmap, evidence-gated:** Realtime (passes the State Test as a Postgres capability — change-feeds over the customer's DB), Analytics (capability first, named Service later), Feature Flags (shared platform product; plan-priced, never per-evaluation), Auth (= customer end-user auth, v4 — distinct from org identity, ADR-0006), Streams (the ADR-038 ladder: Binding first, named Service on evidence).
2. **Execution models (Composer plane)** — named resolutions with stated semantics; extensible per refinement 1; the accepted estimate is the contract.
3. **Infrastructure (architecture plane)** — the State Test, the Services enum `[postgres, valkey, web, worker]`, the substrate. Unchanged, API-honest, never the catalog. (ADR-0003 is the proof: the entire database substrate swapped with zero customer surface change.)
4. **Platform capabilities** — Billing, Identity, Secrets, Observability, Policies, Audit, Organizations; the grammar every product inherits (ADR-0006's governed-resource contract). Unchanged.

## 7 · Companion review (reconciled here, then removed at founder direction)

A second independent review of the same decision existed at `docs/plan/dx-first-review.md`; after reconciliation the founder directed its removal, so its load-bearing content is preserved here. The two reviews agreed on: keep the wedge, keep the enum, keep the pricing construction, AI stays a Binding, no new products without evidence, validate with the five partners next. They differed on form (restatement vs. codifying ADR-040) — resolved by scoping ADR-040 to codify without moving any frozen surface — and on the console re-front, where this review notes the intent catalog is mandated by already-ratified ADR-038/039 regardless, with a measured blast radius of ~8 authority files and no backend. **Absorbed verbatim:** the pre-registered M4 pivot checkpoint (§5). **Evidence points preserved:** the five design partners were already pitched the certainty wedge — re-pitching has a credibility cost with named humans; Supabase holds the outcome-bundle lane (~$10.5B, default distribution inside AI app builders) — entering it on breadth is our weakest axis against the incumbent's strongest; the AI-gateway dead-category evidence stands (ADR-034: funded routers abandoned routing; Cloudflare/Vercel give gateways away); usage-pricing bill-shock is the #1 documented demand signal (wedge review); prior founder rulings on record: "unpredictable usage pricing contradicts Steloit's promise of cost transparency" and "success is now measured by working software and customer feedback — not by additional architectural refinement."
