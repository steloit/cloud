## Outcome

The M7 billing lifecycle state machine (`internal/subscription`), with **status
as the single master state** — the API's `dunning{}` and `wind_down{}` objects
are derived from status + the timestamps, never a second source. The migration
extends the T2.7 inert row with `trial`/`cancelled_at_anchor` and the dunning /
cancel timestamps.

Transitions (guarded; each emits a `subscription.*` billing event to the org
spine): `StartTrial`, `Activate` (payment succeeds — from trial or ANY dunning
state, clearing everything instantly per the billing pack), `FailPayment`
(→ grace), `Cancel` (→ cancelled_at_anchor with `plan_ends_at` = the next anchor,
resources untouched), `Reactivate` (undo before the anchor), and the time-driven
`AdvanceLifecycle` (grace → provisioning_paused at day 7, → suspended at day 21;
the due cancel drops the plan to free at the anchor). **Time is injected**, so
the whole timeline is clock-warped in the integration test — never real waits.

Verified against real Postgres: every one of the six states is reached, the
dunning day-8/day-22 progression fires, payment recovery is instant, cancel →
reactivate and cancel → plan-ends-at-anchor both work, illegal transitions are
refused, and the lifecycle lands on the spine. Live in prod via `RunLifecycle`
(an hourly sweep of `AdvanceAll` over subs in a dunning track or with a due
cancel — the same worker shape as the email outbox). Payment stays external
(T11.4/Stripe): its webhooks call `Activate`/`FailPayment`; this package holds
no provider concept.
