## Why this exists

US-3.8 established that pinned capacity is metered. `web` and `worker` price an
`instances` count, so a pin on them reprices cleanly. **`postgres` and `valkey`
do not** — postgres prices `size`, `storage_gb` and `ha`; valkey prices
`memory_mb`. Neither has a rate for an additional replica.

So a pin on those two products provisions capacity the catalog cannot price.
Under the ratified invariant they are **refused** (422 naming
`override.instances`) rather than provisioned unbillable. That is the correct
resting state, but it removes a shipped operator affordance for the primary
product until a price exists.

## The ratified principle (founder, 2026-07-27)

> 1. Never provision what cannot be billed correctly.
> 2. Never invent commercial pricing in implementation code.
> 3. Restore operator affordances only after the pricing model exists.
>
> The temporary loss of an operator feature is acceptable. Silent commercial
> assumptions are not.

Explicitly rejected during US-3.8: reusing postgres's `ha` rate (1900¢) as a
proxy for one replica. There is no evidence `ha` prices an additional replica
rather than the HA capability as a whole — synchronous replication, failover,
management. Reusing it would silently change pricing.

**This task is founder-owned** because the rate is a commercial decision, not an
implementation detail.

## Scope

- A ratified per-replica rate for `postgres` and `valkey`, in `pricing.json`
  with its rationale recorded.
- The shape schema gains the field, so it becomes part of the contract identity
  (US-3.7) automatically — a replica count someone pays for is a configuration
  they contracted for.
- `estimates.PriceWithInstances` stops refusing those products.
- The refusal test flips to an acceptance test, and the priced path gets the
  same cap + expiry + metering coverage `web` has today.
- Migration: existing services have no replica count. Decide whether an absent
  count means 1 (and therefore prices as today) — it should, or every existing
  service reprices on deploy.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A per-replica rate exists for postgres and valkey with a recorded rationale
  distinguishing it from `ha`.
- [ ] A pin on postgres reprices, clears the hard cap, and meters at the pinned
  rate — the same invariant `web` holds.
- [ ] No existing service changes price as a result (absent count == 1).
- [ ] The replica count participates in the contract identity, so a pinned
  configuration cannot be substituted for an unpinned one.
- [ ] Mutation-verified: removing the new rate makes the pin refuse again rather
  than price at zero.

## Also in scope: the contract must declare the bounds

`openapi.yaml`'s `override.instances` is `{type: integer}` with no `minimum`,
while US-3.8's handler now refuses `< 1` with a 422. The floor exists only
server-side, so generated clients and the console still model `0` and `-5` as
valid and discover the truth as an error.

This task already lists `openapi.yaml` in its `files:` for the postgres/valkey
narrowing, so the `minimum: 1` belongs here rather than orphaned — both copies
(`docs/product/08-api/` and the console's) plus a regenerate. Flagged by US-3.8's
architecture review, 2026-07-27.
