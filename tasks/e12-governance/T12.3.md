## Goal

The ai-assistant policy (enabled | opt_in | disabled) enforced as a STRUCTURAL
guarantee: disabling hides ALL /assistant/* surfaces (404 empty-equivalent),
deletes nothing, re-enables instantly (AI Law 4).

## Read first

- contexts/ai-plane.md — the four laws; Law 4 is the disable-hides guarantee.
- internal/identity/assistant.go — AIAssistantEnabled (the enablement primitive,
  landed with T13.3: closest-wins, project-override aware).
- internal/identity/policy/policy.go — aiAssistantKind (the RBAC 403-narrowing).

## Approach

The enforcement PRIMITIVE and its first consumer (threads) shipped with T13.3.
T12.3 makes the "gate ALL /assistant/*" clause enforced BY CONSTRUCTION rather
than per-handler discipline: `TestEveryAssistantHandlerGatesOnPolicy` reads the
assistant-tagged operationIds from the openapi contract, intersects with the
strict server's include list (the IMPLEMENTED ops), and asserts each handler's
body calls AIAssistantEnabled. A future assistant endpoint (insights, proposals,
postMessage) that forgets the gate fails this test. The ai-plane pack records
the requirement + the 404-not-403 rationale.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] disable gates all /assistant/* (404 empty-equivalent) — enforced by the
  tripwire (every implemented assistant handler must call AIAssistantEnabled);
  behaviorally proven for threads by TestAssistantThreadsDisableHidesNeverDeletes.
- [x] deletes nothing; instant re-enable (Law 4) — proven by the threads test
  (rows survive disable in the DB; re-enable restores verbatim).

## Out of scope

- The unimplemented assistant endpoints (insights, proposals, postMessage) land
  in later E13 tasks; the tripwire will REQUIRE each to gate as it ships.

## Related

AI Law 4 · T12.1 (policy vocab) · T13.3 (AIAssistantEnabled + threads gating).

## Outcome

T12.3 converts the Law-4 "hide all AI surfaces" clause from a per-handler
convention into a compile-adjacent invariant. The primitive
(`AIAssistantEnabled`) and the threads gate shipped in T13.3; this task adds
`TestEveryAssistantHandlerGatesOnPolicy` — a tripwire that parses the
assistant-tagged operations from the openapi spec, intersects them with the
implemented (include-list) set, and fails if any implemented assistant handler's
body omits the `AIAssistantEnabled` call. Two implemented ops (createThread,
listThreads) verified; the five not-yet-built ops (postMessage, listInsights,
updateInsight, getProposal, applyProposal) are skipped until they ship, at which
point the tripwire binds them. The ai-plane pack now records the enforcement
mechanic and the 404-not-403 rationale (Law 4 wants the surface invisible, not
permission-denied) in its mistake bank.


**Review round (approved reviewer + qa agents, run read-only per ADR-0008 —
executed via the .claude/agents/reviewer.md + qa.md instructions since the
harness could not instantiate the named agents; NO Kernel):** both APPROVED the
substance and flagged real tripwire-robustness gaps, all fixed:
- Detection now keys off the `/assistant/` PATH block, not a ±160-char `tags:`
  window — robust to multi-tag `[assistant, ai]` and block-YAML tags (the old
  window could silently drop an op → an ungated handler ships green). Proven by
  TestAssistantOpsSurviveTagFormats.
- Handler bodies are now BRACE-MATCH bounded, not next-func/EOF — a trailing
  package-level helper mentioning AIAssistantEnabled can no longer be miscredited
  to the last handler. Proven by the negative self-test.
- The detector's RED state is now proven: TestGateDetectorHasARedState asserts
  the predicate fails an ungated handler and passes a gated one (a tripwire whose
  failure branch never runs can rot to always-green — QA high).
The Law-4 disable→404→retained→re-enable BEHAVIOR remains covered by T13.3's
TestAssistantThreadsDisableHidesNeverDeletes; this structural tripwire
complements it (which handlers call the gate), no duplication.
