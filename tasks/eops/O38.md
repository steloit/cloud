## The defect

`usage_events` is append-only and correct about that. It has **no idempotency
key**. `id` is a fresh `use_<hex>` per call, so the same span edge emitted twice —
a retry, a replay, a redeployed worker, a support script re-run — inserts twice
and bills twice. `MustEmitSpan` logs loudly and continues on failure, which is
right for not rolling back provisioning state, but it means the recovery path is
"a human reads a log line".

Nothing at the ledger level defends against it. The defense is currently the
emit-once discipline of every present and future call site.

## What the market does, and the window is the interesting part

| system | key | dedupe window |
|---|---|---|
| Metronome | `transaction_id` | **34 days** |
| OpenMeter | CloudEvents `id` + `source` | **32 days** |
| Stripe | `identifier` | ≥24h, scoped honestly to "accidental retries within extremely brief intervals" |
| Lago (Postgres) | `transaction_id` | rejects a duplicate with 422 |

The long windows are deliberate: **one billing period plus slack**, so replaying a
whole period is safe. OpenMeter states the purpose — "so you don't need to store
the idempotency key indefinitely on your backend" — and records what they
rejected: in-memory buffers (no consistency guarantee), bloom filters (false
positives drop events), and ClickHouse `ReplacingMergeTree` ("duplicates can be
present for hours or longer").

Stripe's 24 hours is the outlier and does not protect a replay.

## The shape

A `dedupe_key text` column with a **unique index**, supplied by the caller and
derived from what makes the edge unique — `service_id` + `edge` + the generation
or the edge timestamp — so the same edge computes the same key anywhere. Insert
becomes `ON CONFLICT (dedupe_key) DO NOTHING`, and a duplicate is a no-op rather
than an error, because the caller retrying is behaving correctly.

Postgres holds one billing period comfortably; no separate store is warranted at
this scale, and a unique index is the strongest available guarantee.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Emitting the same edge twice writes **one** row and the second call succeeds.
2. The key is **derived, not random** — two independent callers computing it for
   the same edge agree. A random key would satisfy the column and nothing else.
3. Deleting the unique index makes a test fail. Prove by mutation, not assertion.
4. Existing rows are backfilled or the migration states why they cannot be, since
   they predate the key.
5. A correction is still expressible: the compensating event is a *different*
   edge and must not be deduped against the original.

## Notes

Model-independent — this is a defect under fixed pricing and under ADR-0018's
allocated-resource pricing alike. **The cost of adding it rises with every row
already written**, which is the argument for doing it now rather than with the
pricing work.

## Outcome

**The framing changed during implementation, and it is the better one.** The
defect is not mainly that a retry double-bills — `MustEmitSpan` does not retry,
it logs. It is that **because retrying was unsafe we did not retry**, so a failed
emit was a silent billing *gap* whose recovery path was a human reading a log
line. The key inverts that: once a replay is safe, the emit can be retried and the
gap closes. Prevention of double-billing is the precondition, not the prize.

### `services.status_changed_at` — because a transition had no identity

The key must be derived from **committed state**, or a retry recomputes something
different and dedupes nothing. But the services row carried no per-transition
timestamp, so `(service, edge, from→to)` repeats every time a service cycles
`ready → suspended → ready`.

A key built from that tuple alone would **collapse two genuine transitions into
one and silently drop revenue** — the opposite failure, and a worse one, because
under-billing is invisible in a way double-billing is not. `status_changed_at` is
stamped in the same `UPDATE` as the status, so the two cannot disagree.

    status transitions   <service_id>:<edge>:<status_changed_at>
    reprice close/open   <service_id>:<edge>:reprice:<generation>

A reprice is a desired-state write, so `generation` identifies it; the pair shares
a generation and is separated by the edge.

### Enforcement is the unique index, not the caller

`ON CONFLICT (dedupe_key) DO NOTHING`, and the insert returns rows-affected so a
caller can tell "inserted" from "already there" **without either being an error**.
A duplicate must be a success, or the retry path becomes error handling and nobody
writes it.

An empty key is **refused** rather than defaulted to a random one: a random key
satisfies the column and defeats the mechanism, which is worse than refusing
because it looks like it worked.

### No dedupe window, deliberately

Every metering product bounds dedupe by time — Metronome 34 days, OpenMeter 32,
Stripe ≥24h — because they hold events in a stream store and cannot keep keys
forever. We are in Postgres with a unique index, which is a **stronger** guarantee
than any window, and at this scale the index is free. If `usage_events` is ever
partitioned by period the index becomes per-partition and the window arrives on
its own; that is the moment to revisit.
