## Goal

Two related holes, both about the SAME constant being right for one
multiplication and not for the one that actually happens.

## 1 · The ceiling is a per-service bound; the rollup is per-org

`money.MaxMonthly` is derived as `MaxInt64 / secondsInLongestMonth`, which makes
`rate × one month` exactly fit an int64. But `metering.Rollup` computes
`weighted += secs * rate` **accumulated across every span of every service in
the org for the period** (`rollup.go:67,89`), and `invoice.go:88` sums those.

So `MaxMonthly × secondsInLongestMonth` ≈ 9.223e18 consumes the ENTIRE int64
budget for a single service-month. Two services at that rate for one month give
~1.84e19 and wrap `quota_usage.rate_cents` — the number billing derives charges
from.

Reachable in principle: an org with no budget row is uncapped
(`services.go:41-43`), `Price` accepts ~2.0e9 instances **by design** (asserted,
not accidental), and `openapi.yaml` puts no `maximum` on `shape.instances`. So
two authenticated creates suffice.

`money.go`'s own doc — "the largest monthly amount this platform can carry
through its WHOLE money path without wrapping" — and ADR-0014's derivation
rationale are both **false as written** for the org-wide sum.

**Pre-existing and not worsened by O16**, which bounded the per-line and
per-estimate arithmetic — strictly more than was bounded before. This is the next
layer. Note the severity asymmetry flagged in review: the INGRESS side of this
same class was `critical`, and this is the side that reaches the invoice.

Options (partly a pricing decision, so state it rather than silently picking):
- bound `Rollup`'s accumulator with `money.Sum` so it fails closed rather than
  wrapping — implementation-only, no pricing input needed; or
- divide the ceiling by a stated max-services-per-org and record that number in
  ADR-0014 — that IS a commercial ceiling and needs the founder.

The first is available immediately and is probably the right first move: a
wrapped invoice line is strictly worse than a refused rollup.

## 2 · Nothing ties the constant to the window it is derived from

`TestTheBillingMonthConstantCoversTheLongestRealPeriod` (in
`internal/platform/money`) RE-IMPLEMENTS `AddDate(0, 1, 0)` rather than calling
`metering.periodBounds`, which is unexported and in another package. So it
catches the constant drifting and does NOT catch metering changing its period
WINDOW. Nothing in `internal/metering` references `money.MaxMonthly` or the
seconds constant; only prose ties them.

Add a test on **metering's** side driving the real `periodBounds` across a range
of periods (leap and non-leap), asserting every period's seconds fits what the
ceiling was derived from. That puts the guard on the side the constant does not
control, which was the point of the original test.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A rollup that would exceed the representable range fails closed rather
  than wrapping, and the failure is observable (log + metric or a spine event) —
  not a silent zero.
- [ ] A test in `internal/metering` drives the real `periodBounds` and would go
  red if the period window grew past what the ceiling assumes.
- [ ] ADR-0014's ceiling paragraph states the aggregation bound explicitly.
- [ ] If option 2 is taken, the max-services-per-org number is a founder
  decision recorded in the ADR, not a constant chosen in implementation code.

## Found by

O16's architecture review and independently by its QA review, 2026-08-23. Both
reported it non-blocking for O16: neither is caused by that branch and O16
strictly reduces the exposure.
