## Goal

Two related holes, both about the SAME constant being right for one
multiplication and not for the one that actually happens.

## 1 · The ceiling is a per-service bound; the rollup is per-org

`money.MaxMonthly` is derived as `MaxInt64 / secondsInLongestMonth`, which makes
`rate × one month` exactly fit an int64. But `metering.Rollup` computes
`weighted += secs * rate` **accumulated across every span of every service in
the org for the period** (`rollup.go:67,89`), and `invoice.go:88` sums those.

So `MaxMonthly × secondsInLongestMonth` ≈ 9.223e18 consumes the ENTIRE int64
budget for a single service-month. Two services at that rate for one month give
~1.84e19 and wrap `quota_usage.rate_cents` — the number billing derives charges
from.

Reachable in principle: an org with no budget row is uncapped
(`services.go:41-43`), `Price` accepts ~2.0e9 instances **by design** (asserted,
not accidental), and `openapi.yaml` puts no `maximum` on `shape.instances`. So
two authenticated creates suffice.

`money.go`'s own doc — "the largest monthly amount this platform can carry
through its WHOLE money path without wrapping" — and ADR-0014's derivation
rationale are both **false as written** for the org-wide sum.

**Pre-existing and not worsened by O16**, which bounded the per-line and
per-estimate arithmetic — strictly more than was bounded before. This is the next
layer. Note the severity asymmetry flagged in review: the INGRESS side of this
same class was `critical`, and this is the side that reaches the invoice.

Options (partly a pricing decision, so state it rather than silently picking):
- bound `Rollup`'s accumulator with `money.Sum` so it fails closed rather than
  wrapping — implementation-only, no pricing input needed; or
- divide the ceiling by a stated max-services-per-org and record that number in
  ADR-0014 — that IS a commercial ceiling and needs the founder.

The first is available immediately and is probably the right first move: a
wrapped invoice line is strictly worse than a refused rollup.

## 2 · Nothing ties the constant to the window it is derived from

`TestTheBillingMonthConstantCoversTheLongestRealPeriod` (in
`internal/platform/money`) RE-IMPLEMENTS `AddDate(0, 1, 0)` rather than calling
`metering.periodBounds`, which is unexported and in another package. So it
catches the constant drifting and does NOT catch metering changing its period
WINDOW. Nothing in `internal/metering` references `money.MaxMonthly` or the
seconds constant; only prose ties them.

Add a test on **metering's** side driving the real `periodBounds` across a range
of periods (leap and non-leap), asserting every period's seconds fits what the
ceiling was derived from. That puts the guard on the side the constant does not
control, which was the point of the original test.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] A rollup that would exceed the representable range fails closed rather
  than wrapping, and the failure is observable on BOTH arms — the narrowing one
  and the accumulation one. Only the narrowing arm was pinned at first, and it is
  the one that cannot occur without an int64-exceeding fleet; the reachable arm
  (an unrepresentable or negative `rate_cents`) was silent-deletable — and it does not write at all,
  rather than writing a partial or zero figure over the previous value.
- [x] A test in `internal/metering` drives the real `periodBounds` and would go
  red if the period window grew past what the ceiling assumes.
- [x] ADR-0014's ceiling paragraph states the aggregation bound explicitly.
- [x] **Neither option was taken.** Both of the filed options were wrong:
  `money.Sum` fails closed on arithmetic that is simply CORRECT (two
  service-months is a legitimate business fact), and dividing the ceiling by a
  max-services-per-org is a commercial ceiling requiring the founder. A third
  option removes the need for either: `money.Accrual` carries 128 bits, so the
  accumulation cannot overflow at all and no bound has to be chosen.

## Found by

O16's architecture review and independently by its QA review, 2026-08-23. Both
reported it non-blocking for O16: neither is caused by that branch and O16
strictly reduces the exposure.

## Outcome

**A second type, not a bigger constant.** `money.Accrual` is Σ(rate × seconds) in
128 bits. An accrual is not an amount: bounding it by `MaxMonthly` refuses correct
arithmetic, and bounding it by `MaxInt64` IS the wrap. At 128 bits it takes ~3.7e19
service-months at the ceiling to overflow, so the class is unrepresentable rather
than checked — the same move `Cents` makes, applied to the quantity `Cents` is
multiplied into. `Accrual.Int64()` is the single narrowing point and the only place
that can fail; it fails loudly and `Rollup` then writes NOTHING, leaving the prior
row and its `computed_at` untouched.

`DivSeconds` exists for a reason worth naming: `bits.Div64` **panics** when the
high word is at least the divisor, which is exactly the large-org case. A caller
writing that division inline crashes the process instead of getting an error.

**Eleven mutations RED**, on a throwaway copy with a GREEN baseline asserted before
and after each sweep. Two were survivors first, and both were MY test gaps:
the panic guard (two ceiling service-months lands 111,616 *below* 2^64, so `hi`
was still zero and the panic case was never constructed) and the `accErr` branch
(the overflow test fails at the narrowing step, so it never produces an accErr at
all — a rate above `MaxMonthly` is a different branch). Both closed and re-measured.

**AC 2's guard is now on the side the constant does not control.** The money-side
test re-implemented `AddDate(0,1,0)`, so it caught the constant drifting and never
metering's period WINDOW growing — the half that actually breaks the derivation.
`TestEveryRealPeriodFitsWhatTheCeilingWasDerivedFrom` drives the real
`periodBounds` across 108 periods including leap Februaries.

**The READERS were converted too — and my first attempt at that was wrong twice.**
`invoice.Close`, `mtdSpend` and `billing.SpendToDate` all summed
`quota_usage.rate_cents` with a raw `+=`, and the wrap was real:
`SpendToDate(2900, MaxInt64/2, MaxInt64/2)` returned **-9223372036854772910**.

**The justification I gave for the fix was FALSE.** I wrote that this "is the
number the hard spend cap enforces against". It is not. The cap is
`provisioning.enforceBudget`, which never calls `SpendToDate`, does its own
arithmetic through `money.FromInt`/`Cents.Add`, and already fails closed on an
unrepresentable stored row. `SpendToDate`'s only production callers are the
billing OVERVIEW and `invoice.Close`. Caught on review; corrected in the code
comments, in `budget_http.go` (whose new comment contradicted a correct one ten
lines below it), and here.

**And saturating the INVOICE was worse than the wrap.** Measured: one
`quota_usage` row at -500 — the column is `bigint NOT NULL` with no CHECK, and
`Rollup` is not its only writer — froze `TotalCents` at 9223372036854775807
against Σlines of 30200. `UpsertInvoiceForPeriod` is `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, so
that invoice is permanent. Saturation is right for a figure that must be
RENDERED and catastrophic for one that is FROZEN: an invoice total is a charge,
and there is no safe direction in which a charge can be MaxInt64. `Close` now
sums with a checked addition and REFUSES, because the invariant an invoice owes
is Σ(lines) == total and one that cannot honour it must not exist.

`SpendToDate` keeps saturating for the overview, which has to show something.

**Found and filed, not fixed: O30, and it is worse than the overflow.**
`quota_usage.rate_cents` is written as cent-seconds and read as cents by all four
consumers. Measured end to end through the real code path: one service at
**$24.00/month running for one hour invoices $86,400.00**. No test caught it
because nothing drives `Rollup` and `invoice.Close` in the same test — the invoice
test seeds `quota_usage` directly. Which side moves, and the proration convention,
are pricing decisions (founder ruling of 2026-07-27), so O30 carries a
`NEEDS FOUNDER INPUT` block — and `docs/founder-config.md` §5 now carries the ❓
row, which is the only mechanism that actually interrupts.

Two corrections to that finding, both from review: `usage_http` is on the
**cent-seconds** side (it divides), not the cents side, so it is three readers
against two and not four against one — a distinction that matters because O30 as
first written would have moved the writer to cents and left `usage_http`
reporting $0.00. And `Rollup`'s only production caller is `GetUsage`'s
recompute-on-read, not a scheduler: an unrepresentable accrual therefore makes
`GET /usage` a 500 for that org and period, with no operator correction path
because `usage_events` is append-only. Still the right trade against a wrapped
figure, but the blast radius is recorded rather than implied.
